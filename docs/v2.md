# Lantern v2: design

**Status: built and tested on branch `v2`, not deployed.** This document specified Lantern v2 before any of it was written; [§13](#13-implementation) records how the implementation follows it, and the one place it had to differ. It covers four contract-level changes that v1 lists as its largest gaps ([SECURITY.md §6.4](../SECURITY.md#6-known-limitations), [README roadmap](../README.md#roadmap)):

1. rate-limited opens;
2. an emergency lock;
3. a delay chosen at enrolment;
4. private guardian check-ins.

v2 is a **new contract**. The shipped v1 contract is frozen on Preprod and verified against a fresh compile of its sources, so no byte of it changes. Every circuit below is compiled with compact 0.31.1 and measured. All 13 circuits fit k ≤ 14 ([§8](#8-cost)).

## 0. At a glance

| | Rule | New ledger | Circuits touched | Cost of the change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Rate-limited opens** | At most one live recovery per identity root. Only a current guardian can open, at most once per head per quarter. After each veto the next open waits 1, 2, 4 … 32 days. A recovery that misses quorum within 7 days, or is not finalized within 7 days of its unlock, stops blocking. | `liveRecovery`, `openNullifiers`, `vetoCounts`, `lastVetoAt`; three timeline fields on the record | `openRecovery` (new signature), `approveRecovery`, `vetoRecovery`, `finalizeRecovery`, `rotateGuardianSet` | `openRecovery` 4,657 → 13,447 rows (k = 13 → 14) |
| **2. Emergency lock** | The veto card locks the identity. While it is locked, the gate and `proveHeadOwnership` refuse. Unlocking needs the veto card **and** the identity secret; a finalize to a new device also clears the lock. Veto, rotation and recovery still work while locked. | `locked` | new `lockIdentity`, `unlockIdentity`; `hostGatedAction`, `proveHeadOwnership`, `finalizeRecovery`, `enrollIdentity` | lock 4,812, unlock 9,308; gate +5 rows |
| **3. Configurable delay** | Chosen at enrolment, from 24 h to 90 days, stored per lineage root and inherited by every successor. `finalizeRecovery` enforces it instead of the fixed 72 h. | `recoveryDelays` | `enrollIdentity` (new argument), `openRecovery`, `finalizeRecovery` | enrol 7,529 → 7,673 |
| **4. Private check-ins** | A current guardian proves membership in the current set and writes a nullifier bound to (secret, context, period). A counter for (root, context, period) goes up by one. The ledger shows a count, never which guardian. | `checkIns`, `checkInNullifiers` | new `checkIn` | 12,203 rows |

**One decision goes beyond the brief: opens become guardian-gated.** "At most one live recovery" combined with v1's permissionless `openRecovery` would let any stranger squat the slot and block the owner's real recovery indefinitely. [§3.2](#32-why-a-guardian-must-open) gives the reasoning. The recovering device still holds no secret. The guardian who pays for the open (in v1's story, Seo-yeon) now also proves membership.

## 2. Names and domain separation

Every contract-specific derivation gets a `lantern2:…:v1` domain. A v2 value can then never equal a v1 value, whether in a ledger, in an indexer or in a client that handles both. A guardian who reuses one secret in both deployments gets v2 leaves and nullifiers that are unlinkable to their v1 ones.

v1 already uses a distinct `Bytes<N>` length per domain within each hash family, though not everywhere: v1's approve and veto nullifiers share length 18. v2 **strengthens** the rule so that within each hash family, every preimage has a different length:

| Derivation | Domain string | N | Hash | Preimage after the domain |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| `recoveryIdOf` | `lantern2:rid:v1` | 15 | persistentHash | idCommit, ephemeralPk |
| `approvalNullifierOf` | `lantern2:approve:v1` | 19 | persistentHash | secret, idCommit, rid |
| `vetoNullifierOf` | `lantern2:veto-nul:v1` | 20 | persistentHash | secret (Field), rid |
| `checkInNullifierOf` *(new)* | `lantern2:checkin-nul:v1` | 23 | persistentHash | secret, ctx, period (Uint<32>) |
| `guardianLeafOf` | `lantern2:guardian:v1` | 20 | persistentCommit | secret, ctx; salt |
| `ephemeralPkOf` | `lantern2:eph:v1` | 15 | transientHash | sk |
| `gateNullifierOf` | `lantern2:gate:v1` | 16 | transientHash | secret (Field), nonce |
| `lineageLeafOf` | `lantern2:lineage:v1` | 19 | transientHash | idRoot, member |
| `openNullifierOf` *(new)* | `lantern2:open-nul:v1` | 20 | transientHash | secret, idCommit, period (Uint<32>) |
| `checkInKeyOf` *(new)* | `lantern2:checkin-key:v1` | 23 | transientHash | idRoot, ctx, period (Uint<32>) |

- **Shared on purpose:** `idCommitOf` and `vetoCommitOf` come from the unchanged `identity` module (`lantern:id:v1`, `lantern:veto:v1`). An identity commitment means the same thing in both contracts, and that is the module's reason to exist. **Consequence:** an owner who enrols the *same* secret and salt in v1 and v2 publishes the same `idCommit` in both ledgers, which links them. v2's client helper (`src/v2/identity.js`) therefore derives salts under `lantern2:idsalt:v1` and `lantern2:vetosalt:v1`. The same identity secret then yields a different `idCommit` in v2, and the share set is still the complete recovery kit, because each salt is still a function of its secret.
- **Why the two cheap hashes are transientHash:** `openNullifierOf` sits in `openRecovery` next to the membership proof. A persistentHash (~4,500 rows) there would push it past 2^14. `checkInKeyOf` is a public map key that the client recomputes with the same build's `pureCircuits`. Both inherit v1's caveat about `transientHash` stability across toolchain upgrades ([SECURITY §6.11](../SECURITY.md#6-known-limitations)). An upgrade could at worst let a guardian open twice in one quarter, or split a quarter's check-in count across two keys. `checkInNullifierOf` is persistentHash, like the approval nullifier it mirrors.

## 3. Rate-limited opens

### 3.1 Rules

1. **Guardian-gated.** `openRecovery` proves that the caller's leaf is in the **current** guardian set of the identity's root. The proof is the same membership check as `approveRecovery`: `guardianLeafOf(secret, ctx, salt)` bound to the path, and the path's root in `guardians`. Opening is **not** an approval: the counter starts at zero.
2. **One per guardian, per head, per quarter.** The open writes `openNullifierOf(secret, idCommit, period)`. Binding `idCommit`, the head being recovered, means a successful recovery gives every guardian a fresh quota for the successor. A rogue guardian cannot reset their own quota, because only a finalize (which needs quorum) changes the head. A rotation leaves it alone, and a re-added guardian with the same secret keeps their used quota for that quarter.
3. **One live recovery per root.** `liveRecovery[idRoot]` holds the rid of the latest open. A new open is refused unless that recovery is **dead** ([§3.4](#34-live-and-dead)).
4. **A doubling cooldown after vetoes.** `vetoRecovery` increments `vetoCounts[idRoot]` and records `lastVetoAt[idRoot]` as the later bound of a bracketed time claim. The next open needs `blockTime ≥ lastVetoAt + cooldownSecondsOf(vetoCounts)`:

   | vetoes | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 or more |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | cooldown | none | 1 day | 2 days | 4 days | 8 days | 16 days | **32 days** (bound) |

   The count survives recoveries: harassment of a lineage keeps its level. **A rotation resets it to 0.** A rotation needs both secrets, evicts every guardian, and opens are guardian-only, so whoever was harassing can no longer open.
5. **Expiry.** A recovery stops blocking the slot, and can never finalize, once it misses quorum by `approveBy`, or once it passes `expiresAt` unfinalized.

### 3.2 Why a guardian must open

v1's `openRecovery` is permissionless (SECURITY §5, the liveness oracle). Putting "at most one live recovery" on top of that creates a new attack:

> **Slot squatting.** Mallory, a stranger, opens a recovery for Alice with Mallory's own device key. It can never reach quorum, but it holds Alice's only slot. If Alice vetoes it, the cooldown starts, and when the cooldown ends Mallory and Alice's guardians race to open. Mallory, with a script, wins the race. If Alice has lost her veto card, Mallory's recovery holds the slot until it expires, and Mallory reopens in the same block it dies. **In both cases Alice's real recovery never opens.** v1's attrition surface (§6.4) would have become an availability failure, which is worse.

Every fix that keeps opens permissionless either brings back unlimited parallel opens, or lets the squatter's recovery be displaced, which lets anyone displace the owner's recovery too. The thing a squatter lacks is guardian membership, so v2 asks for it. The recovering device needs a guardian to approve anyway, so asking one of them to open adds no new dependency.

With guardian-gated opens:
- **A stranger can't open at all.** The liveness oracle closes to everyone except the identity's own guardians. Each guardian can probe at most once per head per quarter, and after each veto the doubling cooldown applies.
- **A rogue guardian (fewer than t), with the owner holding the veto card:** the owner vetoes and rotates, which evicts the rogue and resets the cooldown.
- **A rogue guardian, with nobody holding the veto card** (card lost, or the inheritance case of [§5](#5-configurable-delay)): the rogue's recovery can't reach quorum, so it dies at `approveBy`, 7 days after it opens. The rogue can't reopen for that head until the next quarter. Each rogue guardian holds the slot for at most 7 days per head per quarter, or 14 days when two squats straddle a quarter boundary. The honest guardians have the rest.
- **t colluding guardians:** unchanged from v1 (SECURITY §4.3). They can take the identity if nobody vetoes within the delay. Now they get at most t opens per head per quarter, and each veto doubles the wait.

**Rejected alternatives:**
- **A bond on open:** Compact 0.31.1 has no value transfer usable here without a wallet-bound primitive, and `test/authentication.test.js` forbids those.
- **An owner-authorised open:** the owner who lost the device has no identity secret.
- **Letting a newer open displace an older one:** anyone could then kill the owner's recovery.

### 3.3 The timeline of one recovery

All the constants are `export pure circuit`s, so the client and a watcher read them from the same compiled code:

| Constant | Value | Circuit |
|---|---|---|
| open slack | 600 s | `openSlackSeconds()` (unchanged) |
| approval window | 7 days, 604,800 s | `approvalWindowSeconds()` |
| finalize window | 7 days, 604,800 s | `finalizeWindowSeconds()` |
| period | 91 days, 7,862,400 s | `periodSeconds()` |
| delay bounds | 86,400 to 7,776,000 s | `minRecoveryDelaySeconds()`, `maxRecoveryDelaySeconds()` |
| cooldown | table above | `cooldownSecondsOf(vetoes)` |

`openRecovery` brackets the claimed time as v1 does (`lo ≤ blockTime < lo + 600`, with `hi = lo + 600`) and **freezes the whole timeline into the write-once record**:

```
openedAtLo, openedAtHi = hi                       (v1)
unlockAt   = hi + recoveryDelays[idRoot]          finalize needs blockTime ≥ unlockAt
approveBy  = hi + 7 d                             approve needs blockTime <  approveBy
expiresAt  = unlockAt + 7 d                       finalize needs blockTime <  expiresAt
```

The record now carries the exact unlock and expiry times, so a watcher can show them without knowing the constants. That covers the research's "visible recovery timeline" request.

**Period.** Period `p` is the half-open interval `[p·P, (p+1)·P)` of Unix seconds, where `P = 7,862,400`. It is a fixed 13-week quarter counted from the epoch, not a calendar quarter. Period 227 runs from 2026-07-23 to 2026-10-22 UTC. The caller passes `period` as a public `Uint<32>` argument, and the circuit checks it with two block-time predicates:

```
start = (disclose(period) as Uint<64>) * P
assert(blockTimeGte(start),     "period has not started")
assert(blockTimeLt(start + P),  "period has ended")
```

Nobody can claim a future period or a past one. The only freedom is at a boundary, where a transaction built just before it may land just after and be refused, and the client retries. The circuit does no division, since the prover names the period and the circuit only checks it.

### 3.4 Live and dead

When a new open is checked against the slot's current recovery R:

```
dead(R) ⇔ killed(R)
        ∨ retired(R.idCommit)                          -- finalized, or its head was superseded
        ∨ guardianCtx[R.idRoot] ≠ R.ctx                -- rotated out
        ∨ blockTime ≥ R.expiresAt                      -- not finalized in time
        ∨ (blockTime ≥ R.approveBy ∧ approvals[R] < thresholds[R.idCommit])   -- missed quorum
live(R) ⇔ ¬dead(R)
```

**Invariant: `dead(R)` ⇒ R can never finalize, and `dead` is permanent.** Each clause is permanent, and each is refused by `finalizeRecovery`:
- `killed` and `retiredIdentities` are insert-only, and finalize asserts neither holds.
- `usedGuardianCtx` makes contexts globally unique, so a rotated-out context can never become current again, and finalize asserts the context is current.
- Block time is monotone. Finalize asserts `blockTime < expiresAt`.
- Once `approveBy` passes, `approveRecovery` refuses, so the count is frozen below t, and finalize asserts `count ≥ t`.

Each open requires the previous slot holder to be dead. By induction, **at most one recovery per root can ever still finalize.** That is the property that matters, and it also holds under concurrency ([§7](#7-concurrency)).

A recovery **at** quorum after `approveBy` stays live until `expiresAt`, so a finished-but-unfinalized recovery keeps its slot through the whole delay.

### 3.5 Ledger additions

```
export ledger liveRecovery:   Map<Bytes<32>, Bytes<32>>;  // idRoot -> rid of the latest open (overwritten by each open)
export ledger openNullifiers: Set<Bytes<32>>;             // openNullifierOf(secret, idCommit, period)
export ledger vetoCounts:     Map<Bytes<32>, Counter>;    // idRoot -> vetoes; seeded at enrol, reset by rotation
export ledger lastVetoAt:     Map<Bytes<32>, Uint<64>>;   // idRoot -> the later bound of the last veto; seeded 0

struct RecoveryRecord {   // v1's six fields, plus:
  unlockAt: Uint<64>;  approveBy: Uint<64>;  expiresAt: Uint<64>;
}
```

`vetoCounts` is a `Counter`, not a `Uint<8>` level. **The veto only ever increments it and writes `lastVetoAt`, so it binds no read on rate-limit state.** This follows v1's reason for `killed` being a write-only set: the owner's safety action should not contend. The open reads the count.

### 3.6 Circuit changes

```
circuit recoveryIsDead(rid: Bytes<32>, idRoot: Bytes<32>): Boolean {    // not exported
  const rec = recoveries.lookup(rid);
  return killed.member(rid)
      || retiredIdentities.member(rec.idCommit)
      || guardianCtx.lookup(idRoot) != rec.ctx
      || blockTimeGte(rec.expiresAt)
      || (blockTimeGte(rec.approveBy)
          && approvals.lookup(rid).lessThan(thresholds.lookup(rec.idCommit) as Uint<64>));
}

export circuit openRecovery(idCommit: Bytes<32>, ephemeralPk: Bytes<32>, period: Uint<32>): Bytes<32> {
  // v1, unchanged: enrolled, not retired, rid not yet used, bracketed lo/hi
  ...
  const idRoot = idRoots.lookup(disclose(idCommit));
  const ctx = guardianCtx.lookup(disclose(idRoot));
  // NEW: the opener is a guardian of the CURRENT set
  const secret = guardianSecret();  const path = guardianPath();
  assert(path.leaf == guardianLeafOf(secret, ctx, leafSalt()), "path does not bind to this guardian leaf");
  assert(guardians.checkRoot(disclose(merkleTreePathRoot<20, Bytes<32>>(path))), "guardian not in tree");
  // NEW: one open per guardian per head per period
  assertCurrentPeriod(period);
  const onul = openNullifierOf(secret, idCommit, period);
  assert(!openNullifiers.member(disclose(onul)), "guardian already opened a recovery this period");
  openNullifiers.insert(disclose(onul));
  // NEW: the cooldown after vetoes
  const vetoes = vetoCounts.lookup(disclose(idRoot)).read();
  assert(vetoes == 0
         || blockTimeGte((lastVetoAt.lookup(disclose(idRoot)) + cooldownSecondsOf(vetoes)) as Uint<64>),
         "cooling down after a veto");
  // NEW: one live recovery per root
  assert(!liveRecovery.member(disclose(idRoot))
         || recoveryIsDead(liveRecovery.lookup(disclose(idRoot)), idRoot),
         "a recovery is already live for this identity");
  const unlockAt = (hi + recoveryDelays.lookup(disclose(idRoot))) as Uint<64>;
  recoveries.insert(disclose(rid), RecoveryRecord { ...v1 fields...,
    unlockAt: disclose(unlockAt),
    approveBy: disclose((hi + approvalWindowSeconds()) as Uint<64>),
    expiresAt: disclose((unlockAt + finalizeWindowSeconds()) as Uint<64>) });
  approvals.insert(disclose(rid), default<Counter>);
  liveRecovery.insert(disclose(idRoot), disclose(rid));
  return disclose(rid);
}
```

**Implementation note: the `vetoes == 0` clause.** The design prototype asserted only `blockTimeGte(lastVetoAt + cooldownSecondsOf(vetoes))`. A rotation zeroes `vetoCounts` but leaves `lastVetoAt`, and `lastVetoAt` is the last veto's *later* bound, up to 600 s after the veto's block. So an open right after "veto, then rotate" still waited out the rest of that slack, and §3.9 case 9 ("an open lands with no wait") failed by up to ten minutes. With the clause, no vetoes means no wait and no read of `lastVetoAt`. It costs 44 rows (13,403 → 13,447, still k = 14).

`||` and `&&` short-circuit in circuits, and a ledger read inside an untaken branch is not performed. We checked this under 0.31.1 in the simulator, and it compiles to ZKIR. So `liveRecovery.lookup` is never reached for a root with no open yet.

The other changes, by circuit:

| Circuit | Change | Message |
|---|---|---|
| `approveRecovery` | Add `assert(blockTimeLt(rec.approveBy), …)` after the killed check. Everything else is v1's. | `approval window has closed` |
| `vetoRecovery` | After the veto-commit check, add `assert(!retiredIdentities.member(rec.idCommit), …)`. After inserting into `killed`, bracket `claimedNow()` exactly as open does, then `vetoCounts.lookup(rec.idRoot).increment(1)` and `lastVetoAt.insert(rec.idRoot, hi)`. | `identity retired`; `claimed time is in the future`; `claimed time is too far in the past` |
| `finalizeRecovery` | Replace `openedAtHi + recoveryDelaySeconds()` with `rec.unlockAt`. Add `assert(blockTimeLt(rec.expiresAt), …)` after it. | `timelock has not elapsed` (unchanged); `recovery expired` |
| `rotateGuardianSet` | Add `vetoCounts.insert(idRoot, default<Counter>)` (a reset). | none |
| `enrollIdentity` | Seed `vetoCounts[idCommit] = default`, `lastVetoAt[idCommit] = 0` ([§4](#4-emergency-lock) and [§5](#5-configurable-delay) add more). | none |

**The veto's time anchor.** The cooldown runs from the veto's later bound `hi ∈ (blockTime, blockTime + 600]`. The prover is the owner, and lying can lengthen the cooldown by at most the slack, never shorten it below the veto block. Anchoring at the open instead would let an attacker whose recovery was vetoed late reopen at once.

**Why the retired check is new in `vetoRecovery`.** v1 lets anyone holding an old identity's veto card "veto" that identity's finalized recovery, which does nothing. In v2 that call would also bump the successor's cooldown. So v2 refuses: **a retired card has no effect on the successor.**

### 3.7 How it composes with v1's rules

- **Lineage and succession:** the slot and the cooldown are keyed by `idRoot`, so they follow the lineage. After a finalize, the old recovery is dead (its idCommit is retired), and a recovery for the successor can open at once. `vetoCounts` is not reset by a finalize.
- **Rotation:** kills the live recovery (the context changes), resets the count, and evicts every guardian, **including from opening**, because opens check the current context.
- **Veto:** still owner-exclusive, per recovery and replay-proof as in v1. It now also feeds the cooldown, and it refuses on a retired identity.
- **The gate:** unaffected.
- **v1's "rotation kills a quorum":** holds unchanged, and now also frees the slot.

### 3.8 What becomes public

| New public fact | New information? |
|---|---|
| "A current guardian opened this" | New, and it is the point: v1 said "anyone". *n* is already public (SECURITY §5). The opener is 1-of-*n*, exactly like an approver. The open discloses a historic guardian root and an open nullifier, nothing more. |
| `openNullifiers` | Counts opens, which `recoveries` already shows. Unlinkable across quarters, across heads, and to approvals and check-ins (different domains and inputs). |
| `liveRecovery`, `vetoCounts`, `lastVetoAt` | Derivable in v1 from `recoveries`, `killed` and transaction times. Now explicit. |
| `unlockAt`, `approveBy`, `expiresAt` | Derivable from `recoveryDelays` and the constants. |

**The liveness oracle shrinks** from "anyone, as often as they pay" to "the identity's own guardians, once per head per quarter each, with doubling waits after each veto". It does not disappear: a guardian can still ask the question. That residual goes into SECURITY §5 when v2 ships.

### 3.9 Adversarial cases a test must cover

These go in `test/v2-ratelimit.test.js`. The refusal messages are the circuit's own.

1. A second open while one is live is refused (`a recovery is already live`), from a *different* guardian and with a different device key.
2. A stranger with no leaf cannot open. A guardian of another identity cannot open for this one: its leaf binds a different context (`does not bind`). A forged secret against a real path fails the same way.
3. A guardian evicted by rotation cannot open (`does not bind`: the current context changed).
4. A guardian opens at most once per head per period (`already opened a recovery this period`), even with a new device key. In the next period (`advance(P)`) it can. After a successful finalize, the same guardian can open for the successor in the same period (a new head).
5. A future period (`has not started`) and a past period (`has ended`) are refused.
6. After one veto, an open at `lastVetoAt + 1 d − 1` is refused (`cooling down`) and one at `lastVetoAt + 1 d` is accepted. Assert that `lastVetoAt` equals the veto's `hi`.
7. The cooldown doubles: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 32 days (the bound). Check `cooldownSecondsOf(0…7)` directly, and check three levels end to end.
8. A veto claiming a future time is refused, and so is one claiming a time more than 600 s stale.
9. A rotation resets the count: after rotating and re-adding guardians, an open lands with no wait. A finalize does **not** reset it.
10. Slot release, with each boundary checked at t − 1 and t:
    - vetoed: after the cooldown;
    - finalized: at once;
    - rotated out: at once;
    - expired: at `expiresAt`;
    - missed quorum: at `approveBy`.
    A recovery **at** quorum past `approveBy` still holds the slot until `expiresAt`.
11. An approval at or after `approveBy` is refused (`approval window has closed`).
12. A finalize at or after `expiresAt` is refused (`recovery expired`). At `unlockAt` it succeeds.
13. **Never two finalizable recoveries:** after a missed-quorum or expired R1 lets R2 open, R1 can neither gain approvals nor finalize.
14. **Concurrency:** two opens by two guardians, proved from the same state. The second's transcript, replayed after the first applies, is refused on a read mismatch. Use the pattern of `test/concurrency.test.js`.
15. A retired identity's veto card cannot veto (`identity retired`), and so cannot move the successor's cooldown.
16. Opening is not approving: after an open, `approvals[rid] = 0`, and the opener can still approve once.
17. **Privacy:** the open's public transcript holds no guardian secret, salt or path sibling. Use `assertNoLeak`, with its positive control.

## 4. Emergency lock

### 4.1 Rules

- **`lockIdentity(idCommit)`** proves the veto card, exactly as a veto does, and sets `locked[idRoot] = true`. The identity must be enrolled and **not retired**.
- **While locked**, these refuse with `identity is locked`:
  - `hostGatedAction`;
  - `proveHeadOwnership`, which is v1's "I currently control R". Leaving it open would leave a gate for a thief to use.
- **While locked**, these still work:
  - `proveSuccession`: a public fact, not an authorisation;
  - veto, rotation, `addGuardian`;
  - open, approve and finalize: the owner's way out of a compromise *is* a recovery;
  - `checkIn`.
- **`unlockIdentity(idCommit)`** needs the identity secret **and** the veto card, and sets `locked[idRoot] = false`.
- **`finalizeRecovery`** writes `locked[idRoot] = false`. A recovery to a new device clears the lock, and the successor starts unlocked.
- Lock and unlock are **idempotent**: locking twice, or unlocking an unlocked identity, succeeds. This avoids a read.

### 4.2 Why both secrets to unlock

The lock is for the moment the owner learns their device, and so their identity secret, is in someone else's hands. The thief holds the identity secret and not the card, so **the thief cannot unlock**. Someone who copied the card but not the secret can lock (a nuisance) but cannot unlock. Only the owner, holding both, undoes a lock without a recovery. The usual path is: lock, then recover with the guardians, and the finalize clears the lock and installs a fresh identity secret and veto card.

### 4.3 Ledger and circuits

```
export ledger locked: Map<Bytes<32>, Boolean>;   // idRoot -> locked; seeded false by enrollIdentity
```

It is a `Map<…, Boolean>` seeded at enrolment, not a `Set` with insert and remove. Every write is then a plain insert, and every reader does one `lookup`, with no member check and no conditional.

```
export circuit lockIdentity(idCommit: Bytes<32>): [] {
  assert(enrolled.member(disclose(idCommit)), "identity not enrolled");
  assert(!retiredIdentities.member(disclose(idCommit)), "identity retired");
  assert(vetoCommits.lookup(disclose(idCommit)) == vetoCommitOf(vetoSecret(), vetoSalt()),
         "locking requires the veto secret");
  locked.insert(disclose(idRoots.lookup(disclose(idCommit))), true);
}

export circuit unlockIdentity(idCommit: Bytes<32>): [] {
  assert(idCommitOf(identitySecret(), idSalt()) == idCommit, "not the identity owner");
  assert(enrolled.member(disclose(idCommit)), "identity not enrolled");
  assert(!retiredIdentities.member(disclose(idCommit)), "identity retired");
  assert(vetoCommits.lookup(disclose(idCommit)) == vetoCommitOf(vetoSecret(), vetoSalt()),
         "unlocking requires the veto secret");
  locked.insert(disclose(idRoots.lookup(disclose(idCommit))), false);
}

// hostGatedAction: after the Gate_ownershipHolds assert (which, through `descends`, makes rootIdCommit a genuine root)
  assert(!locked.lookup(disclose(rootIdCommit)), "identity is locked");
// proveHeadOwnership: after proveSuccession(idRoot, head)
  assert(!locked.lookup(disclose(idRoot)), "identity is locked");
// finalizeRecovery: last line
  locked.insert(disclose(rec.idRoot), false);
// enrollIdentity: seed
  locked.insert(disclose(idCommit), false);
```

`ownergate.compact` is unchanged. The lock is a fourth fact that v2 asserts next to the module's three, with its own message so tests can tell the two apart. A host that imports `ownergate` against v2's ledger must read `locked` too. SECURITY §8's host-integrator guidance gets that sentence when v2 ships.

### 4.4 How it composes

- **Lineage:** keyed by root, so one lock covers the whole lineage. A lock set before a recovery is cleared by that recovery's finalize.
- **Succession:** the old head's card cannot lock or unlock (`identity retired`), so **a retired card can never lock the successor**. A lock proved before a finalize but landing after it is refused on replay: its read of `retiredIdentities` flips ([§7](#7-concurrency)).
- **Rotation and veto:** unaffected either way. Rotation does not clear a lock, because the owner holding both secrets can unlock directly.
- **The attested host** (`host.compact` with `src/host/snapshot.js`): v1-only and out of scope. A v2 snapshot would have to leave locked roots out of the live set ([§11](#11-limits-and-follow-ups)).

### 4.5 What becomes public

A single bit per root. A lock tells the world that the owner believes their secret is compromised, and proves the owner holds the veto card and is alive. That is a proof of life, but a voluntary one, unlike the liveness oracle. An unlock proves possession of both secrets. No secret or salt appears in either transcript.

### 4.6 Adversarial cases a test must cover

These go in `test/v2-lock.test.js`.

1. The card holder locks. Then:
   - `hostGatedAction` is refused (`identity is locked`);
   - `proveHeadOwnership` is refused (`identity is locked`);
   - `proveSuccession` still passes.
2. The identity secret alone cannot lock (`locking requires the veto secret`).
3. A thief holding only the identity secret cannot unlock (`unlocking requires the veto secret`). The card alone cannot unlock (`not the identity owner`). Both unlock, and the gate works again.
4. Nothing else is blocked. While locked, every one of these succeeds:
   - veto, rotation, `addGuardian`;
   - a guardian's open, the approvals, the finalize;
   - `checkIn`.
5. A finalize clears the lock. The successor passes the gate with its new secret, and the old secret is refused as retired.
6. The **old** card cannot lock or unlock the successor (`identity retired`). Another identity's card cannot lock this one.
7. Locking A does not lock B. Lock and unlock are idempotent.
8. A gate call refused while locked does **not** consume its nonce: after an unlock, the same nonce succeeds.
9. **Concurrency:**
   - A gate action proved before a lock lands is refused on replay: the lock wins.
   - A lock proved before a finalize lands is refused on replay, so no stale lock reaches the successor.
   - A finalize proved before a lock and landing after it clears that lock. This is the documented behaviour: a finalize always leaves the lineage unlocked.
10. **Privacy:** no veto secret or salt in `lockIdentity`'s public transcript, and neither secret in `unlockIdentity`'s. Use `assertNoLeak`, with its positive control.

## 5. Configurable delay

### 5.1 Rules

- `enrollIdentity(idCommit, vetoCommit, threshold, delay)` takes `delay: Uint<64>` in seconds and asserts `86,400 ≤ delay ≤ 7,776,000`, refusing with `delay below the minimum` or `delay above the maximum`.
- The delay is stored once, as `recoveryDelays[idRoot]`. At enrolment the root is the idCommit. The map is insert-once with no setter, so it never binds a contended read.
- **Successors inherit it for free.** `finalizeRecovery` carries `idRoots[new] = rec.idRoot`, and every open reads `recoveryDelays[idRoot]`. No copy is needed. v1 copied `thresholds` because that map is keyed per commitment.
- **The open freezes the delay into the record** as `unlockAt = hi + delay`, and `finalizeRecovery` asserts `blockTimeGte(rec.unlockAt)`. v1's `recoveryDelaySeconds()` (72 h) is gone from the contract. The client keeps 72 h as its default (`src/v2/timeline.js`, `DEFAULT_DELAY = 259200`).

```
export ledger recoveryDelays: Map<Bytes<32>, Uint<64>>;   // idRoot -> seconds, write-once at enrolment
```

A lying opener can still shave at most the 600 s slack. The lock runs from `hi`, as in v1, so a finalize lands no earlier than `delay` after the block that opened the recovery.

### 5.2 The inheritance profile

**A long delay, combined with the owner watcher, is a private dead man's switch.**

1. **Setup.** The owner enrols with `delay = 90 days`. Their guardians are the heirs, or an executor and the heirs. `t` is chosen as usual. The owner runs the watcher, the site's watch page, which reads `recoveries` for the root and alerts on any open. The guardians check in each quarter ([§6](#6-private-guardian-check-ins)), so the owner sees "3 of 3 guardians checked in this quarter" without learning who.
2. **While the owner is alive.** Any open is visible for 90 days. The owner vetoes it with the card, and the cooldown then doubles for the next. The owner can lock the identity if they think their device was taken.
3. **After death or incapacity.** An heir who is a guardian opens a recovery for the executor's device. `t` guardians approve within 7 days. Nobody vetoes for 90 days. The device finalizes within the following 7 days and holds the identity. Every host that gates on the root keeps working for the heir.
4. **What the heirs never hold** before the switch fires: the identity secret, unless `t` of them pool shares, which is SECURITY §4.3 and is exactly what the 90-day window and the veto answer.

**Honest costs of the profile:**
- **The delay is public**, so a 90-day `recoveryDelays` entry marks an identity as using an inheritance setup. Hiding it would need a committed delay and an in-circuit range proof against block time.
- **The delay is lineage-wide and fixed**, so the owner's own recovery after an ordinary lost phone also waits 90 days.
- **An owner offline for longer than the delay loses the veto race**, as with any timelock.
- **A rogue heir can delay but not block.** Guardian-gated opens, the 7-day approval window and the one-per-quarter quota bound them ([§3.2](#32-why-a-guardian-must-open)).

### 5.3 How it composes

- **Rotation:** does not touch the delay.
- **Veto:** unaffected. A longer delay only lengthens the veto window.
- **The lock:** independent.
- **Rate limit:** the delay sets `unlockAt`, and therefore `expiresAt`, so a finished recovery under a long delay holds the slot for `delay + 7 days` at most.

### 5.4 What becomes public

`recoveryDelays[idRoot]`, and through it every record's `unlockAt` and `expiresAt`. v1 published one global 72 h; v2 publishes each identity's choice.

### 5.5 Adversarial cases a test must cover

These go in `test/v2-delay.test.js`.

1. Enrolment boundaries:
   - 86,399 is refused (`below the minimum`);
   - 86,400 is accepted;
   - 7,776,000 is accepted;
   - 7,776,001 is refused (`above the maximum`).
2. The record's `unlockAt` equals `openedAtHi + delay`, and `approveBy` and `expiresAt` are as in §3.3.
3. For a 24 h identity and a 90-day identity, a finalize at `unlockAt − 1` is refused (`timelock has not elapsed`) and one at `unlockAt` is accepted.
4. **Inheritance:** after a finalize, the successor's next recovery uses the root's delay. No circuit takes a delay after enrolment.
5. Two identities with different delays do not interfere.
6. A lying prover (`claimedNow = blockTime − 599`) cannot finalize earlier than `delay` after the opening block. This is the v1 test, ported.
7. **The inheritance profile, end to end:**
   - a 90-day identity;
   - the owner vetoes a premature open;
   - the next open waits the 1-day cooldown;
   - the approvals come within 7 days;
   - no veto;
   - a finalize at day 90 plus the slack succeeds, and one at `expiresAt` would have been refused.

## 6. Private guardian check-ins

### 6.1 Rules

`checkIn(idCommit, period)`:
- `idCommit` may be any enrolled commitment of the lineage. It maps to the root, and the context is the root's current one. Guardians are not asked to track the current head.
- It checks the period as in §3.3.
- It proves the caller's leaf is in the **current** guardian set, with the membership proof `approveRecovery` uses.
- It writes `checkInNullifierOf(secret, ctx, period)`, so each guardian checks in **once per context per period**. Because the nullifier binds the secret, not the leaf, a guardian with two leaves still counts once. That is v1's "cannot inflate a quorum with extra leaves", applied again here.
- It increments `checkIns[checkInKeyOf(idRoot, ctx, period)]`.

The owner, or anyone, reads that one counter and compares it with the guardian count *n* for the current context. *n* is already public: count the `addGuardian` calls since the last rotation. The result is "2 of 3 guardians checked in this quarter", **and never which two**.

```
export ledger checkIns:          Map<Bytes<32>, Counter>;   // checkInKeyOf(idRoot, ctx, period) -> count
export ledger checkInNullifiers: Set<Bytes<32>>;

export circuit checkIn(idCommit: Bytes<32>, period: Uint<32>): [] {
  assert(enrolled.member(disclose(idCommit)), "identity not enrolled");
  const idRoot = idRoots.lookup(disclose(idCommit));
  const ctx = guardianCtx.lookup(disclose(idRoot));
  assertCurrentPeriod(period);
  const secret = guardianSecret();  const path = guardianPath();
  assert(path.leaf == guardianLeafOf(secret, ctx, leafSalt()), "path does not bind to this guardian leaf");
  assert(guardians.checkRoot(disclose(merkleTreePathRoot<20, Bytes<32>>(path))), "guardian not in tree");
  const nul = checkInNullifierOf(secret, ctx, period);
  assert(!checkInNullifiers.member(disclose(nul)), "guardian already checked in this period");
  checkInNullifiers.insert(disclose(nul));
  const key = checkInKeyOf(idRoot, ctx, disclose(period));
  if (!checkIns.member(disclose(key))) { checkIns.insert(disclose(key), default<Counter>); }
  checkIns.lookup(disclose(key)).increment(1);
}
```

The key binds the context, so **a rotation starts a fresh count**: the new set's health, with the evicted set's check-ins excluded. The context survives a recovery, so the count continues across a finalize within the same quarter. The key also binds `idRoot`, which is redundant given that contexts are unique, but keeps the key self-describing, as the brief asks.

### 6.2 What becomes public, and what does not

- **Public:** the per-(root, context, quarter) count; the fact that some current guardian of this identity was active at this block; the historic guardian root the proof used; the period.
- **Not public:** which guardian. The nullifier is a hash of the guardian's secret, and it changes with the period and the context, so one guardian's check-ins are **unlinkable across quarters**. They are unlinkable to the guardian's approvals (`lantern2:approve:v1`, binding a rid) and opens (`lantern2:open-nul:v1`, binding the head) as well, because each uses a different domain and different inputs.
- **Metadata**, as for approvals (SECURITY §5, §8):
  - timing, such as checking in right after the owner's reminder;
  - the freshness of the disclosed root, since the client should prove against the current root, as `src/witnesses.js` does;
  - whatever the fee payment reveals (§4.2b).
- **A new signal:** the count tells everyone which identities have inattentive guardians. That is a target-selection hint for social engineering, and it is the price of a public health check.

### 6.3 How it composes

- **Rotation:** evicted guardians cannot check in, because the leaf is bound to the current context. The counts restart under the new context.
- **Succession:** the count is keyed by root and context, both unchanged by a finalize.
- **Rate limit and lock:** independent. A lock does not block a check-in.
- **Approvals and opens:** a check-in is neither an approval nor an open, and uses different nullifier sets.

### 6.4 Adversarial cases a test must cover

These go in `test/v2-checkin.test.js`.

1. Two guardians check in, and the counter reads 2. The transaction's arguments are only `(idCommit, period)`.
2. The same guardian twice in a period is refused (`already checked in this period`). This holds **even from a second leaf with the same secret and a different salt**.
3. In the next period the same guardian checks in again, and the old period's count is unchanged.
4. A future period (`has not started`) and a past period (`has ended`) are refused.
5. A non-guardian is refused (`does not bind`, or `not in tree`). A guardian of identity B cannot check in for A, and cannot move A's count.
6. After a rotation:
   - an evicted guardian is refused;
   - a guardian re-added under the new context checks in;
   - the new key's count starts at 0, and the old context's count is untouched.
7. Across a recovery, a check-in through the successor's commitment and one through the root increment the **same** counter, so the second from the same guardian is refused.
8. **Unlinkability:**
   - one guardian's check-in nullifiers for two periods differ;
   - they differ from that guardian's approval and open nullifiers;
   - the public transcript contains no secret, salt or path sibling. Use `assertNoLeak`, with its positive control.
9. **Concurrency:** two guardians' *first* check-ins of a period, proved from one state. The second is refused on replay, because both created the counter, and it succeeds when re-proved. Two later check-ins proved from one state both apply, because increments don't conflict.
10. A lock does not block a check-in.

## 7. Concurrency

v1's rule is that hot paths bind no read that a concurrent honest action can flip (`test/concurrency.test.js`). v2 keeps it:

| Circuit | New reads it binds | Who can flip them | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| `approveRecovery` | none (`blockTimeLt` is checked against the block, not state) | nobody | Concurrent approvals still commute. Port v1's Spike B tests. |
| `finalizeRecovery` | none new (`unlockAt` and `expiresAt` come from the immutable record); writes `locked` | nobody | A finalize proved at quorum still applies after a later approval. Port it. |
| `vetoRecovery` | `retiredIdentities[rec.idCommit]` | a finalize | Ordering decides, as in v1 §6.7: a finalize first makes the veto moot. The veto writes the counter and `lastVetoAt` without reading them. |
| `openRecovery` | the slot, the dead-check reads, `vetoCounts.read()`, `lastVetoAt` | another open, or a veto | Intended: this is how one-live holds under races (§3.9 case 14). An open is a rare path, and a refused one is simply re-proved. |
| `hostGatedAction`, `proveHeadOwnership` | `locked[root]` | a lock or an unlock | Intended: the lock wins (§4.6 case 9). |
| `lockIdentity`, `unlockIdentity` | `retiredIdentities`, `vetoCommits` | a finalize | Intended: no stale lock after a recovery. |
| `checkIn` | `checkIns.member(key)` (false → true once per period) | the period's first check-in | Only the first check-in of a period can conflict, and it retries. |

## 11. Limits and follow-ups

- **t colluding guardians still win** if nobody vetoes within the delay (SECURITY §4.3). v2 bounds how often they can try, lets the owner pick a longer window, and lets the owner freeze the identity at hosts while they respond.
- **A copied veto card is now worth more to a thief.** In v1 it could veto every recovery. In v2 it can also lock the identity at hosts and push up the cooldown. The owner unlocks with both secrets, but the thief can lock again. **The fix is a small follow-up we have not designed in detail:** `rotateVetoCommit(idCommit, newVetoCommit)`, needing both secrets.
- **The delay is fixed per lineage.** An inheritance setup also slows the owner's own recovery after an ordinary loss. A follow-up is `changeDelay`, needing both secrets and applying only to recoveries opened after it.
- **Each guardian gets one open per head per quarter.** With *n* guardians, a lineage gets *n* attempts per head per quarter. A mistaken open, for example with the wrong device key, costs one attempt and holds the slot until `approveBy`, or until `expiresAt` if it was approved. The owner's veto frees it sooner, at the price of a 1-day cooldown.
- **Guardian-gated opens are a semantic change.** A v1 client that lets the phone open for itself does not work against v2. The phone asks a guardian, which is what v1's own story already does.
- **Attested hosts:** `host.compact` and `src/host/snapshot.js` are v1's. A v2 snapshot would have to leave locked roots out of the live set. We have not built one.
- **Devnet:** a v2 story on a local chain needs its own flavour that lowers `minRecoveryDelaySeconds()`, as v1's `devnet/flavour.mjs` lowers line 120. It is a stretch goal, and v2 is not deployed anywhere.
- **Block-time monotonicity** is assumed, as in v1, for the "dead is permanent" invariant (§3.4).

**Where the implementation differs from the design above:**

- **No vetoes means no cooldown** ([§3.6](#36-circuit-changes)). The prototype's cooldown check ran from `lastVetoAt` even at a count of zero, so after "veto, then rotate" an open still waited out the rest of the veto's 600 s slack. `openRecovery` now asserts `vetoes == 0 || …`: +44 rows, still k = 14.
- **One ported test runs its race the other way round.** v1's "colluders who pooled shares cannot stop the owner recovery finalizing" opened the colluders' recovery *beside* the owner's. v2 allows one in flight, so the colluders open first and reach quorum, the owner vetoes, and after the 1-day cooldown an honest guardian opens the owner's. Every v1 refusal is kept, and the colluders' second open is refused outright (`already live`).
- **The three concurrency cases of §3.9, §4.6 and §6.4 live in `v2-concurrency.test.js`**, with the replay harness they share, rather than in the per-feature files.
